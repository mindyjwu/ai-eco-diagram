About the AI Money Map

Who actually pays for AI?

You do, a few dollars at a time. Your Amazon orders, your feed, your meals, your trips, your classes, your paycheck, your side hustle, your doctor and your chatbot all run on AI. This map follows that money down through platforms, labs, clouds, chips, factories, power plants and rockets, to a quartz mine in North Carolina, so you can see the whole ecosystem and where you might fit in it.

How to read it

1 · Start from the overview26 layers sit in five zones, from what you spend and consume at the top to raw materials at the bottom. Each row shows its best-connected logos, and the arcs on the right show how many links run between layers.
2 · Zoom to open a layerScroll or pinch, or click a layer chip. The row spreads out to every company, then shows names and tickers as you zoom closer.
3 · Click a companyA panel shows what it makes, how it’s doing and where the opportunity is. Amber lines: who depends on it. Teal: what it depends on. Dashes move the way money flows.
4 · Use the lensesFilter to public stocks, private companies, ◆ chokepoints (parts the world can’t easily replace) or non-company items. Press / to search.

Every layer on this map was once someone’s bold bet. A food company ended up holding the key film inside AI chips. A rocket company rewrote launch costs. The next one could be yours: be ambitious, be curious, and look for the layer others are ignoring.

Caveats

Educational, not investment advice. Descriptions reflect my knowledge to roughly mid-2026 and some details will have moved. “How they’re doing” is qualitative on purpose; use the live-quote and website links in each company’s panel before acting on anything.

Lines show typical, publicly known relationships, simplified. They are not a list of contracts and some are indicative. Logos are fetched from each company’s own website; trademarks belong to their owners.

Spot an error or a missing company? Tell me and I’ll add it. The Layers view on the map has the same data as a scrollable list, which is also the keyboard-friendly way to explore it.
